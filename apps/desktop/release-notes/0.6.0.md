What changed since 0.5.0.

This one is about the hours you aren't watching. Althar can now start work on a schedule, sit out a dropped connection and carry on by itself, fix the checks its pull requests fail, check how much of your plans is left before it starts something, and tidy away the folders and servers it no longer needs. And for the evenings, there's a dark mode.

## New

- **Automations.** A project's new Automations view holds work that starts by itself: a nightly dependency check, a Monday look at open pull requests, a morning triage of new issues. Ask the coordinator for one, start from one of those three, or write your own. Each run is an ordinary task you can open. Schedules follow your computer's clock, at most hourly, while Althar is open. Runs use your plans, never an account paid per use, and one runs at a time, after everything else. A slot missed while the computer slept runs once on waking, or not at all; you never come back to a backlog of them. A run never pushes to the default branch, and an automation's first review waits for you before it posts.
- **How much of your plans is left.** A gauge in the window's bar shows it, and Ctrl+U (⌘U on a Mac) opens each agent's windows and when they reset. Althar reads them for Claude Code, Codex, Kimi Code and Grok Build, and acts on them: new work starts on the account with the most room, and a step whose agent is nearly out goes by the project's usage-limit rule before it starts, not after it fails halfway. A step already running is never stopped.
- **Drop in any file or folder,** not only images: a PDF, a log, a CSV, a zip, a whole folder. Drop it, paste it or pick it with Add files, in a task or the coordinator's chat. Up to 10 at a time, 50 MB each and 100 MB in all. They're copied in beside the agent's work and never committed. What you send the coordinator goes on with the task it starts: on the task's first message, and to its lead.
- **Leads show you pictures** whenever you ask, not only when a step ends: screenshots of what they built, captioned in the thread, opening full size.
- **Run any branch or pull request,** from the Run menu's "Run a branch or pull request…", or by asking the coordinator. Althar fetches it with its own connection to the code host and runs it in a copy of its own. Your checkout isn't touched, and nothing is left behind when it stops.
- **Open the code an agent talks about.** When a thread mentions `Runs.ts:359`, a file or a function, it becomes a chip. Point at it for a peek at the lines; press it and the file opens beside the conversation, scrolled to the lines the agent meant. From there you can see what the task changed in it, copy its path, or open it in your editor. Alt+↓ and Alt+↑ (⌥↓ and ⌥↑ on a Mac) step through every reference in the conversation.
- **A task as a graph.** A third view beside Conversation and Outputs: Graph, or press G. Each agent gets a lane, each step is a card saying how long it took and what it came to, and a step waiting on you can be answered right on its card.
- **Dark mode.** A warm dark, made from Althar's own paper rather than a cool grey. Settings › Appearance › Light or dark: Light, Dark, or follow your computer. Light is still where you start.
- **Leads and the coordinator can change issues, not only draft new ones:** move, close, reprioritise, assign, relabel or retitle them on your trackers. Each change comes as a card for you to apply or discard. Linear can do all of it; GitHub, GitLab, Jira and Trello do what their APIs allow and say plainly what they can't. When a task's pull request opens, Althar offers to move its issue to review, and when it merges, to close it. To skip the cards: Project settings › General › Change issues without asking.
- **Several Linear workspaces.** Connect each one with Add a workspace, then bind a project to one, and to a team in it if you like, in Project settings › Code and issues.
- **The coordinator reads pull requests and check logs** for itself: state, checks, reviews and comments, and a failed check's whole log. On Claude Code it also runs everyday commands like `git fetch` or `git tag` without asking you, in a sandbox that can't touch your checkout or reach the network.
- **Leads have a few more tools:** reinstall a worktree's dependencies, start and stop the project's dev server, take screenshots of pages running on this computer, and rerun a check that failed by chance on their own pull request.
- **Merge through a merge queue.** On a GitHub branch with a merge queue, Merge puts the pull request in the queue. The task then says "In the merge queue" on the board, the home and its change until the pull request lands. If the queue sends it back, because a check failed there or it conflicts with what's ahead of it, the thread says why and the task comes back to Needs you.
- **Send now** on a queued message, beside Edit. It interrupts the agent, goes first, and the rest of the queue keeps its order.

## Improved

- **Failing checks go back to the lead, even after you've looked.** When a task's pull request fails a check, its lead takes it: what its own change broke, it fixes and pushes; a check that failed by chance, it runs once more; what's broken on the default branch too, it hands to you with a line saying so. Meanwhile its card says "Fixing failing checks". A fix waits for your push if you've written on the task since, or if the branch had commits you hadn't pushed.
- **A finished task stops what it started.** When a task merges, is marked done or is abandoned, its agents stop, and so do the servers they left running and its runs. One Mac had kept 20 processes from five finished tasks, about 490 MB, one of them 21 hours after its merge. Althar also stops what it lost track of, after it starts and every five minutes, and only ever what it marked as its own.
- **Finished tasks' folders are removed after a while.** A task folder with its dependencies can take a couple of gigabytes, and they only ever piled up. Now a review's copy goes right away, a merged change's after 3 days, a done quick task's after a week and an abandoned one's after 2 weeks, sooner when the disk is nearly full. The branch stays, its last commit is saved first, and anything with changes not committed or something still running is left alone. The folder comes back by itself when the task needs it again. To change the wait or turn it off: Settings › Work on this computer › Remove finished tasks' folders.
- **Reviews start from a plan you can change.** Ask the coordinator to review a pull request and you get the usual plan card first: its reason, the countdown, Hold and Start now, and the model and effort to review with. Only agents that can be kept to reading are offered. Automations' reviews still start at once.
- **Every error has a way out.** A signed-out agent says so plainly, with Sign in again, which opens Settings on that agent with its sign-in already started. A step whose agent is signed out goes Stuck at once with Sign in again, instead of failing twice first. A full conversation offers Compact, and a pull request on a code host you haven't connected offers Connect.
- **A project has a task's bar.** One row: the project's mark and name, one chip for what needs you or how many run, its five views, Run, and ⋯ with the repositories and tracker it's made of. The board gets about 80 px back, and the top of the window no longer jumps between a project and its tasks.
- **Things move where they come from.** Menus and popovers grow from what opened them, a card slides from lane to lane on the board, a new message rises into place, switches slide. It's quick, and never on what you do many times a minute. With reduced motion on, things only fade.
- **Running means running.** On the board, Running counts only the tasks an agent is working on right now. A task waiting for its next turn moves to the top of Up next and says what it continues with, as in "Continues · Follow-up". Work paused for a usage limit or the connection gets a group of its own below the line. A task whose lead stopped, because you declined a command or interrupted it, goes to Needs you with Carry on.
- **Merging settles at once.** A task merged from its view leaves Needs you as soon as the code host says it's merged, not several seconds later.
- **Up next drags smoothly.** Take hold of a row anywhere: it follows the pointer, the others slide aside, and a long list scrolls when you hold it at the edge. Alt+Shift+↑ and ↓ (⌥⇧↑ and ↓ on a Mac) on a row's grip move it one place.
- **One chip above a task's composer** says what's behind it: why it waits and where it is in line, the server it left running, the pull request it follows. Click it for the detail, with Stop and Move to the front of the line.
- **Servers have names.** What a lead left running shows as `vp dev` at `localhost:5173`, not a command line cut off halfway. Click the address to open it, or read what it printed.
- **Switching project tabs is instant,** and so is going back to Home, and you land where you left off: same view, same scroll. Busy threads open faster too: with 20,000 past permission answers on file, reading a thread's page went from half a second to under a millisecond. Typing in a long task no longer redraws its whole history. And with many merged tasks still on disk, opening a task or the coordinator no longer waits seconds: with 40 of them, reading the board went from almost half a second to under a fifth.
- **Lighter on memory** over a long day. Althar no longer holds on to every thread, tool call and web answer for as long as it runs: keeping watch over a thousand threads now takes about 4 MB, where it took 57.
- **Resolve conflicts automatically, dependably.** Althar checks each open task's branch against its default branch itself, every couple of minutes and whenever a pull request merges, instead of waiting for the code host to notice. When it can't hand a conflict to the lead, the card says why, with Resolve now.
- **Far fewer "Althar can't tell what this command does" cards.** Most of them were commit messages and pull request descriptions written with a quoted heredoc, which Althar now reads as the plain text they are. When a project rule refuses something, the agent hears that Althar's rules refused it, so it no longer tells you that you declined.
- **What can't be undone always asks now, even where a project allows everything:** a command that pushes to the default branch or deletes it, force-pushes, deletes a branch that isn't the task's, or changes or deletes work in another checkout or outside the task's folder. Everything else really is allowed, a lead's own scripts included: what a Python or Node script does inside isn't read, and is trusted as the agent is. No agent can read `~/.ssh`, `~/.aws`, `~/.npmrc`, `gh`'s sign-in or the like any more.
- **With everything allowed, a lead's ordinary work doesn't stop for a card.** Editing files with a script, using a variable or a loop, or deleting what isn't there goes ahead. Of the 54 such cards one Mac saw in a week, 53 would now have gone ahead.
- **A refused tool call says so on its own row:** who refused it and why, in a few words, instead of a notice repeating the whole command.
- **Leads lose fewer turns to their sandbox.** Each project works out which commands run outside it from the start (browsers, Electron, test runners) and which hosts need no asking (its code host, its package registries, your trackers). See and edit both in Project settings › Rules › Sandbox.
- **The coordinator runs loops.** Its read-only commands may use `for`, `if`, `&&` and variables wherever every command in them only looks, so syncing every repository is one line. A refused command no longer gets a turn of its own to say why, so the coordinator doesn't answer you twice.
- **Leads draft an issue only when you ask for one.** Anything else they find goes in their report in a line, for you to decide. And they look at the project's other tasks first, so nothing is drafted for work another task is already doing.
- **Outputs for a branch without a pull request** says what the remote doesn't have yet: "2 commits not pushed", or "Uncommitted changes in 3 files". It asks the remote itself, so a push made from elsewhere counts. Its one main button opens a draft pull request, a stopped task's too, and Push sits quietly beside the count. A stopped task's header says Stopped, as the board does.
- **A card's main button sits in the same place on every card:** its own row at the bottom, under what the card says, and no card has more than one violet button.
- **A calmer task view.** The ⋯ menu has one line per row, grouped by what you'd do. Where the task stands is a single chip in the header, with Done and Keep for later inside it.
- **Quieter all round.** The model picker leaves out agents you haven't installed, and no longer adds who it hands the conversation to. The load and plan peeks drop "Memory is tight" and the words beside their gauges. A board card's foot becomes a tidy list when it doesn't fit on one line, and a merge conflict's note names three files at most. Up next's divider stays on one line, however narrow the board. The coordinator's chat names who keeps the places once, under the newest waiting card. A new project opens on Home, and its repositories show without a branch and date on every row. Segmented controls slide between options.
- Claude Code's adapter is 0.99.0 and Codex's is 2.3.0.

## Fixed

- **A dropped connection no longer leaves a step Stuck.** The step says "Waiting for the connection", gives its agent's place to other work, and carries on by itself once you're back. While the computer is offline, the window's bar says so and nothing new starts. A service that keeps failing is tried three more times before the step asks for you, and every Stuck step now has Try again.
- **Folder sizes you can trust.** In Settings › Remove finished tasks' folders, a folder's size is what removing it frees. Copies of bun's cache used to count in full, so a folder could show 2 GB and free 15 MB. Totals say "Measuring…" until every folder is measured, and Freed says what really went.
- **A stopped task with a conflict shows as stopped,** not as running with a clock counting the days it stood still. Its card says what stopped it and when, and Carry on picks its step up again, merging the default branch in first.
- A merged pull request no longer says that no checks have run yet.
- Stopping a lead just after Althar pushed its merge keeps the merge's checks yours: when they fail, Althar no longer starts the lead again to fix them.
- Althar starts each agent once to read its models as it opens. Two parts of the window asking at the same moment could start it twice.
- When you ask a review's lead to change its review, the review card moves to the end of the thread, where you are, instead of changing far up it.
- A task could end up waiting behind itself: its review queued for a place while the task's own dev server held one. The review now starts in the place the task already has.
- The load meter counted as little as a fifth of what Althar's work really used. It now counts all of it: test runs that come and go between readings, servers an agent left behind, and Althar itself.
- Removing a Linear key and adding it back no longer leaves the project's issues behind.
- When the keyring can no longer open a saved sign-in, as can happen in the Linux Flatpak, the connection asks you to sign in again, instead of calling GitHub unreachable with a raw error.
- A sign-in you cancel stops there: it no longer opens a browser page for it a moment later. On Windows, Codex hears the cancel too.
- On Windows, a document path that climbs out of a task's folder is refused, as it is elsewhere.

Tell us what breaks: Report a problem in the app, or https://github.com/AltharIDE/althar/issues
