# The homes lab

The chip (A) is the one picked; Manage homes, its dialog, is three takes below. Four takes on #982's homes (a desktop window connected to a personal home over SSH), on the whole desktop app in the browser: its router, tabs and real screens, on made-up data with no runtime. `world.ts` is main's side, made up: the saved homes (hetzner-01, Studio Mac mini), each window and how its connection stands, a check that answers after 1.5 s (a host with `down` in it refuses), a connection that opens the home's window. `data.ts` gives each home its projects (hetzner-01 has ledger and infra). One import seam in `vite.config.ts` gives `TabsFrame.tsx`, `HomeView.tsx`, `StartView.tsx` and #982's `HomeConnections.tsx` the direction's parts (`seam/`); everything else is the app's own.

```bash
cd apps/desktop && ../../node_modules/.bin/vp dev --config lab/homes/vite.config.ts
```

At `http://localhost:6181`. The bar along the bottom, or its keys, swaps the direction in place: `0` as #982 has it, `A`–`D` the four; `1`–`3` open the chip's Manage homes, three ways. `W` brings the next window to the front (this computer's, or a home's once one is open; × closes it), `X` drops the front window's connection, `F` makes the next connection fail, `M` saves many homes, `E` empties a home of projects, `N` narrows the window to the app's narrowest, `T` is dark. ⌘⇧H, Ctrl+Shift+H off a Mac, is the File menu's Homes…, as #982 adds it. The address keeps it all, and opens a state for a screenshot: `?d=c&at=home&open=checked`, `?d=d&w=hetzner&at=project&conn=dropped&theme=dark` (`m`: 1–3, the Manage dialog; `pick`: Manage's home in view, such as `studio`; `open`: homes, connect, checked, refused, manage; `conn`: connecting, reconnecting, dropped, failed, refused; `at`: home, project). `node lab/homes/shoot.mjs` takes every direction's states into `shots/` (outside an agent's sandbox: Chromium won't start in it on a Mac).

## The directions

- **0 · As #982.** A Homes link on the Start view and a Homes button at the bar's end; one dialog lists the homes, with the connection form in it. A home's window shows the launch screen while it connects and the open-failed screen where it can't; a dropped connection is a dialog over the window, and Reconnect reloads it. What SSH can't do is spelled out in a home's empty Start view.
- **A · Chip.** A home's window has its name as one chip at the bar's end, beside the meters; this computer's has a plug once a home is saved. The chip's menu is the chooser. Connecting is one dialog: the host, Check host, then the home's identity with Copy and the line that compares it on the host, then its name. A dropped connection turns the chip red with Reconnect beside it; the window stays as it was. No line lists what SSH can't do.
  *Smallest change from #982 and nothing covers the work; but the cue is small, so it's easy to lose track of which window is which, and a drop is quiet enough that a press can fail before you notice.*
- **B · Home tab.** The window's first tab is its home: Home in this computer's window, the home's name and a server in a home's, and nothing else in the bar says so. A chevron on that tab opens the switcher. Connecting is a dialog that names what it's doing (reaching the host, starting althar-host, reading its home) and then shows the identity as the host's terminal prints it. A dropped connection is a strip under the tabs that tries again by itself (after 3, 10, then 30 s) with Reconnect now, then waits for the person. A home's window offers no + for a new project; the switcher's note says why.
  *The cue sits where you already look and adds no chrome, and the retry gets through a blip without you; but "Home" now means two things, and retrying by itself is new work in main (#982 reconnects only on the person's press).*
- **C · Homes on Home.** Homes are places, so they live on the Home screen, in a band at its foot: this computer and each home as a card, the window's own lifted as its tab is. Connecting opens in that band, the host on the left and what the check found on the right, with no dialog. The Home tab carries the home's name, as in B. A dropped connection is a card at the window's foot with Reconnect; on Home the window's own card says it. What SSH can't do is one line on a home's Home.
  *Easiest to find, and the identity gets room; but the band costs Home some height for everyone with a home saved (one quiet link before), and going to another home from a project goes through Home (⌘⇧H takes you there).*
- **D · Palette and tint.** ⌘⇧H, or the bar's Homes, opens a palette like ⌘K's: every home, found as you type; type a host that isn't saved and it offers to connect it, then checks it in place. A home's window is told apart by colour: its bars take the home's ink, always the same for that home, with its name in that ink at the bar's end. A dropped connection greys the whole window, readable but not pressable, and one sheet offers Reconnect or Close window. What SSH can't do is the palette's foot.
  *Quickest by key, and the strongest answer to "which window is this", even in Mission Control; but colour is new to the chrome (the inks are the projects' too), greying the window is heavy for a blip, and a palette alone hides connecting from someone setting up their first home.*

## Manage homes (the chip's)

Each lets you rename a home (at once), change its connection (checked first, and kept only where the host still has the same home; a host with another home can be saved beside it instead), see and copy its identity, and forget it. Forget acts at once with Undo: it only drops the saved connection, and a window open on the home carries on until it's closed, as #982's main has it. Rename, a changed connection, saving a checked home without opening it and Undo are calls #982's main doesn't have yet (`world.ts` makes them up).

- **1 · Rows that open.** The homes as the chip's menu lists them; a row opens in place to its name, host and options, identity and Forget, set apart at its foot. Open sits on a row's end as it's hovered.
  *One dialog, one list, nothing to learn; but an open row pushes the others down, and with many homes the list scrolls with a form in it.*
- **2 · List and details.** Homes down the left, as Settings lists its sections; the one picked on the right, whole: its name as the title (changed in place), its connection, its identity with Check now (asks the host whether it still has this home), and Forget at the foot in the danger colour. Starts on the window's own home.
  *Room for everything, and Check now answers "is this still my home?" before you connect; but it's the biggest dialog for what is mostly a list of two or three, and the most to build.*
- **3 · Lines and a menu.** One line a home; pressing it opens it, as the chip's menu does. The rest is in its ⋯ menu: Rename turns its name into a field where it is; Edit connection turns the dialog to that home's connection, with Back; Copy home ID; Forget.
  *Smallest and closest to #982's dialog, and the list stays a list; but everything but opening is a step away in a menu, and the identity shows only when you edit.*
