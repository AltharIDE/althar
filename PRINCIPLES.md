# Principles

What Althar is, and stays, whatever else changes. [THESIS.md](THESIS.md) argues why Althar should exist, and [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md) sets the standard its code is held to. These are the product's own rules: the test every feature, screen, setting and word on screen has to pass.

A change that goes against one of them is wrong as it stands. Where a principle itself should change, that is a decision, recorded like any other in [`docs/decisions`](docs/decisions).

## Open source

Nobody should be locked in to a vendor to keep their own work: not a developer, not an organisation, and that includes being locked in to us. So Althar is open source, under the [Apache License 2.0](LICENSE). Read every line, fork it, run it. If we disappear, your setup doesn't.

Everything one person needs to work in Althar on their own is free and open, and always will be.

- **Trust you can check.** How Althar runs your agents, what it keeps and what it sends are in the code, not in a promise.
- **Your work stays yours.** What Althar keeps of a project is in a form you can read and take with you.

## Everything on screen earns its place

Althar is calm. Each screen has a strong hierarchy: what matters most reads first, and the rest stays quiet or out of sight. A control, a count, a line of text or a colour is there because the person needs it then, not because Althar knows it or can do it.

- **Nothing just because it's available.** Detail is there when you go looking for it, and not before.
- **Nothing that says nothing.** No control that does nothing, no decoration that means nothing, no copy that sells. Words on screen state facts plainly.
- **Everything added costs something.** A new thing on a screen makes the rest of it louder or quieter. It has to be worth that.

## Violet means it needs you

Violet is the one colour that asks for you. A permission, a question, a stuck task, work ready to accept: when Althar needs a person, it's violet, and when nothing is violet, nothing needs you. Plan the next task, get on with something else, or sit back.

Althar doesn't reach for your attention without a reason. It does the opposite, and hides what would otherwise take it. An agent at work is a quiet spinner, not a stream of everything it reads and runs. What you care about is what the work produces: the evidence, the changes, the questions. The rest is kept, and opens when you go looking.

- **Violet is only for a decision that waits on a person.** Never for decoration, never for progress, never for the brand.
- **Cobalt is work in motion,** and asks nothing of you.
- **No pings for progress.** A notification, a count on the Dock, the edge round the notch: each is for a call that's yours.

## Built for focus

Althar is there to make you as effective as you can be, and what makes a person effective is focus. Agents that are working and need nothing from you shouldn't wear it down. Watching a transcript scroll by, checking whether something has finished, being told it's still going: each costs focus and gives nothing back.

When something does need you, switching to it should be quick and switching back cheap. The call comes to you where you are, with what you need to decide in hand, and leaves you where you were.

- **Answer where you are.** A permission is allowed from the edge, without leaving the app you're in.
- **A call says what it is and why,** so you can decide without reading back through the work.
- **Back to where you were.** Going back to a project or a task picks up where you left it.

## On top of what you already have

Althar sits on top of what you already use and own: your coding agents, your plans and API keys, your repositories, your code host, your tracker, your editor. It slots in beside them, and doesn't try to replace any of them.

Althar is an IDE, but not a code editor. It isn't a code host, a code intelligence platform, a model or a model gateway, and doesn't want to be.

- **No model of ours, and no gateway.** Agents sign in with their own tools, on the plans you already pay for. Nothing of ours sits between you and the model, and no tokens are resold.
- **No new home for your code.** Repositories stay where they are, pull requests open on your code host, and work comes in from your tracker.
- **Hand over to what does it well.** Where a tool you have already does a job, Althar uses it rather than building its own.

## Beautiful

You sit in front of your tools for hours a day, every day. Althar should be something you enjoy sitting in front of, and come to love. That comes from care in the small things (type, spacing, light, motion, the words), because together they are how a working day feels.

## Sensible defaults

Althar is opinionated. It comes tuned for how good engineering work goes, so it works well before you change anything, and it makes choices rather than handing them to you as settings. A setting exists only where people genuinely differ.

Sensible is the word that matters. Opinionated never means arbitrary: each default is the choice a careful engineer would make, and leaving it alone is right for most people. Another maker's model reviews each change, agents start in a mode where they ask, and pushing stays yours, until you say otherwise.
