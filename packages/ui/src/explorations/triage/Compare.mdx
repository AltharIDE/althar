# Triage checkpoint

A design exploration, not a shipped feature. After a little groundwork, a task's lead has one triage moment. Usually it is a
**checkpoint**: its approach in a line or two and what it is assuming, which carries on by itself when its time runs out unless you
correct something, as the plan card does. Only when a wrong guess would be expensive does it **ask**, and the task waits on you. Asking
should feel like the exception, and an empty checkpoint should feel normal, so a lead is never pushed to invent something to ask.

Everything here is Storybook only: fixtures, no wiring. Each direction has a sheet of every moment in light and in dark, side by side
with the board, and a **Try** story whose clock runs.

- [A. Plan-like card](?path=/story/explorations-triage-checkpoint-a-plan-like-card--light): a card in the thread like the plan card,
  numbered assumptions with No on each, the plan's clock with Hold and Continue now.
- [B. Inline line](?path=/story/explorations-triage-checkpoint-b-inline-line--light): the approach in the lead's own words, then one
  quiet line, "Assuming 3 things · continues in 1:42 · Review", which opens to the list.
- [C. Board-first](?path=/story/explorations-triage-checkpoint-c-board-first--on-the-board): the running card opens, in place, to the
  assumptions, so a checkpoint is settled from the board. Seen here on Meridian's board, with two at once.
- [D. On the brief](?path=/story/explorations-triage-checkpoint-d-on-the-brief--light): most assumptions are readings of what you asked
  for, so they are marked on your words, with the readings under the brief.
- [Coordinator: decided and left](?path=/story/explorations-triage-checkpoint-coordinator-decided-and-left--light): the plan says what
  was settled with you and what it leaves to the lead, so the checkpoint holds only the rest.

## What every direction keeps

These held up in all four, so they are not what the choice is about.

- **No new lane, no new step.** The task stays in Running. Its card says what the first step is doing: _Looking around_, then
  _Checkpoint · 3 assumptions · continues in 1:42_, then _Implement_ as usual. Triage is the start of Implement, not a pip on the track.
- **A checkpoint is ink, not violet.** It doesn't wait on you, so it isn't a call and isn't in Needs you. Held, it still isn't: you
  stopped it, so the card says _held by you_.
- **A question is a call.** Violet, in Needs you as its own card, and the running card says _Waiting on you_. It says why it asks
  rather than assumes, in one of three words the lead must pick from: a wrong guess **redoes the work**, it **reaches outside** (partners,
  customers, production), or it **can't be undone**. Anything else becomes an assumption. What it also assumes rides along and carries
  on with the answer.
- **Althar keeps the clock, not the screen.** Unlike the plan card's, it runs whether or not anyone is looking; otherwise a checkpoint
  nobody saw would block the work. The clock waits only while you write a correction, or when you hold it. It is announced once, not
  every tick, and with reduced motion nothing ticks or drains: it says _continues at 11:06_.
- **What it assumed is kept.** Once it carries on, the card's foot says _3 assumed · 1 corrected_ and the thread keeps a line that opens
  to the list. No still works afterwards, as a message to the lead. The pull request ends with the list (below).

What pushes a lead to invent things is a shape that looks unfinished when it is empty. A's numbered card does that most; B and C have no
shape when empty; D asks every assumption to point at the words or the file it is about, which makes an invented one easy to spot.

## Recommendation

**B, the quiet line, with two things taken from the others.**

1. **Each assumption says where it comes from**, as D's readings do: the words of the brief it reads, or the file or note it rests on.
   It is one line of text in B's list, it makes a vague assumption visible as vague, and it is what the pull request needs anyway.
2. **The coordinator's plan says what is decided and what is left to the lead.** It shrinks the checkpoint before it exists, and makes an
   empty one the expected result of a good plan rather than a lead that didn't try.

B makes an empty checkpoint cost nothing and a full one cost one line, keeps violet for the one question that earns it, and leaves the
board as it is: one button per card, saying where the task is and how long until it carries on. A is the clearest to read but the
heaviest to live with, and its shape asks to be filled. C is the strongest for someone running many tasks from the board, but it breaks
the card's single button and makes cards change height while their clocks run; worth revisiting as B's card opening into the dock's
peek rather than in the lane. D is the best explanation of an assumption and the weakest place to put the clock, at the head of a thread
that may be long.

## Open

- **Two minutes**, where the plan card has its own, shorter wait. Per project, like the other rules? Shorter for a small task?
- **Does the lead wait during the countdown, or carry on and fold corrections in?** These prototypes wait; carrying on would make a
  correction a steer and cost rework.
- **A held checkpoint stays out of Needs you** here, because you held it. If held ones get forgotten, that is the first thing to change.
- **Dark is provisional.** The kit has no dark theme yet; these sheets use one scoped to the exploration, made from the terminal's
  sheets and the island's cobalt and violet. What opens in a portal, like a tooltip, stays light.
- **Words.** _Checkpoint_, _Assumed_ and _Hold_ on a checkpoint need glossary entries before anything ships.
